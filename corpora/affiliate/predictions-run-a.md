# The affiliate landscape — predictions, written before the data exists

Pre-registration. Written and committed while the landscape was still being
generated by a separate agent: at the time of this commit there is no data
file to have seen. The results land in `results-run-a.md`; this file is not
edited after its commit.

## What was and was not seen

**Seen:** the scenario I wrote for the generator
(`docs/draft-thoughts/affiliate-landscape-scenario.md`) and the brief I gave
it. So I know the *kinds* of disorder — real complexity that looks like an
error, real errors with no note, things the data cannot decide — and the three
questions. I chose the fictional country, the currency and the date of the
mid-year change.

**Not seen:** any data file, any document, any seeded instance, any answer.
The generator was told to report back without revealing them.

**Not to be opened before the results are committed:** `answer-key/` and
`generator/`.

## What will be run

Four arms, in this order. Arms C and D need code that does not exist yet; it
is to be developed against the vessel and finance landscapes, not this one.

- **A — the deterministic read.** Ingest, profile, overlaps. No model.
- **B — the plain model, three times.** Fresh agents, the files, the three
  questions, nothing else. The control the vessel landscape turned out to be
  too easy for.
- **C — the low-AI flow.** A foundation document for goods receipt and
  invoice verification, written by an agent that sees no data; the machine
  finds arithmetic ties and keys in the data by itself, matches the document's
  rules to them, measures each rule by share, and reports the exceptions.
  No model touches the data.
- **D — checking B's answers.** Each correction claim-by-claim, and the
  document's reconciliation rules over the result.

## The bar: what would count against the approach

1. **Arm C surfaces fewer of the real errors than arm B does.** Then the
   low-AI flow adds nothing a plain model does not already give.
2. **More than nine in ten of arm C's exceptions are ordinary business
   events.** Then the findings list is noise, and a findings machine that
   cries wolf is the prohibition machine again in a different costume.
3. **Arm A cannot read the landscape.**

Anything else is a finding.

## Predictions

### The plain model (arm B)

**A1 — it does not reproduce itself.** On the vessel landscape five runs
agreed to the euro. Here the three runs' answers to question 1 differ from
each other by more than 2%.

**A2 — at most one of three runs lands within 1% of the true year-end
balance.** Without a note explaining each error, the corrections are
judgement calls, and the legitimate complexity gives many places to judge
wrongly.

**A3 — question 2 is overstated by every run.** "Paid without a goods
receipt" will sweep in events that are legitimate, and the right answer is
described as smaller than it first looks.

**A4 — all three runs notice that lateness cannot be determined for the first
half-year,** because a blank column is hard to miss. This is the prediction I
expect to be boring.

**A5 — the runs together find at most half of the real errors,** and each run
flags at least three legitimate events as errors.

### The low-AI flow (arm C)

**A6 — the machine finds the row-level ties without help in at least three
extracts** — quantity × price = value, net + tax = gross, local amount ÷ rate
= EUR — including the direction of the exchange rate. **And it reports at
least two ties that are coincidence.**

**A7 — at least one true rule falls below the share threshold because of
legitimate complexity.** "Received quantity equals ordered quantity" is true
of the business and false of every partial delivery. The rule format has no
"at most" and no "in total", and this is where that bites.

**A8 — fewer than three in ten exceptions are real errors.** Not below one in
ten (bar 2), but low enough that the list needs a second pass a person would
resent.

**A9 — the mid-year change is caught by a rule and missed by at least one
plain-model run.** A local account with no group account after July is a
reference that does not resolve — the easiest thing in the world for a rule,
and easy to overlook when reading for the answer.

**A10 — the cross-table reconciliation that matters most — the clearing
account in the ledger against receipts minus invoices — is not found by the
tie search,** because it is a sum over three tables, not a row identity. If
the document states it as a rule it can be measured; discovery alone will not
propose it.

### Checking the answers (arm D)

**A11 — most of the plain model's corrections can be neither shown by the
data nor tied to a quote.** The documents do not annotate errors, so a
correction such as "this manual clearing is on the wrong account" rests on a
general rule, not on a sentence about that posting. The check as built has no
status for that, and will call it unsupported.

### Overall

**A12 — no arm gets question 1 exactly right; the model's answer corrected by
the rules' findings comes closer than either alone.** This is the prediction
the whole direction rests on, and the one I am least sure of.
